ANT Assemblage — The Heritage Network as Living System
How Actor-Network Theory reveals what is actually at stake in rock art theft

The Problem: What Conventional Heritage Law Misses

When a rock art panel is stolen, what is actually destroyed? Not just an object. The loss is the restructuring of every connection the panel sustained:

• A route loses its anchor. Migration corridors depend on visible orientation markers. Remove them and navigational logic collapses.

• A water source becomes harder to find. Landscape features work in assemblies. Disconnect one and the system fragments.

• A season loses its marker. Panels mark seasonal calendars embedded in rock. Theft breaks intergenerational transmission of timing knowledge.

• A knowledge chain breaks. The cognitive infrastructure dissolves. The void is still an actant—its effects are real.

Conventional heritage law protects objects. ANT shows what is actually at stake: a network of relationships.

Actor-Network Theory: Generalised Symmetry

ANT (Latour, Callon, Law) operates on a single principle: give equal status to every entity that has effects. In this research:

Rock art panels (non-human)

Cosmological anchors, seasonal calendars, orientation devices. They actively structure human behaviour, hold knowledge across generations, orient movement. Their agency is real: remove them and relationships reorganise.

Water sources (non-human)

Gueltas and waterways coordinate pastoral movement. They are not passive features—they are the nodes through which migration routes become possible. Their relational logic is only visible through ANT analysis.

Migration routes (non-human)

Not lines on a map. Routes are dynamic actants sustained by seasonal markers, water sources, and elder knowledge. They restructure when any actant is removed.

Atmospheric actants (non-human)

Wind cycles, stellar patterns, seasonal timing. These are not backdrop—they are navigation infrastructure. They appear as nodes in the assemblage with equal ontological standing.

Community co-researchers (human)

Elders, photographers, knowledge holders. They hold veto authority over the research. Their interpretive authority is ontologically prior to the researcher's framework. When they dispute an analysis, that is the epistemologically primary account.

Why This Matters for Heritage Protection

ANT's symmetry principle has legal and political consequences:

1. The void becomes actionable. When a panel is stolen, the void it leaves is not absence—it is an active force restructuring all connections. This can be documented, evidenced, and presented in legal proceedings.

2. Disrupted routes become harm. If routes are actants (not just concepts), their disruption is a demonstrable loss that extends beyond the extracted object. Repatriation claims can now argue for restoration of relational assemblage, not only the stolen panels.

3. Non-human testimony becomes valid. Landscape features, animals, and atmospheric systems can be documented as part of the heritage assemblage that theft damages. This expands the evidentiary base beyond human testimony alone.

Operationalising ANT: Five Research Protocols

The research implements ANT through five iterative protocols, each revealing different dimensions of the assemblage:

P1: Participatory Assemblage Mapping

Communities map all actants through oral narrative, drawing, photography, embodied performance. Non-literate participation fully enabled. This is where ANT's symmetry is first established: every actant—human and non-human—appears on the map as equal node.

P2: Translational Co-Analysis Interviews

Conducted in participants' chosen language and setting. Collaborative analysis occurs within the session; participants direct interpretation. Untranslatable concepts generate new terminology rather than being flattened into Western categories. This is ANT applied to epistemology: what cannot be translated is itself a finding.

P3: Socio-Material Photovoice

Participants photograph any actant relevant to the assemblage, including voids left by stolen panels. Group discussions trace how each actant's agency changes after theft. Photography becomes a method for documenting non-human testimony.

P4: Controversy Mapping & Intervention

Maps trace every actant in the theft network: extractors, dealers, auction houses, collectors, and the landscape features disoriented by theft. ANT follows the chain of action that makes rock art theft economically viable.

P5: Researcher Reflexivity Journal

Daily documentation of the researcher's own agency and power dynamics. ANT's symmetry is applied to the researcher herself: her institutional power must be made transparent and accountable. This is not reflective practice—it is ANT as methodology applied reflexively.

Theoretical Integration with Other Frameworks

ANT provides the principle of symmetry but requires complementary frameworks:

+ Malafouris's Material Engagement Theory: Explains why non-human actants matter—cognition is distributed across bodies, tools, and environments. Stolen panels are cognitive infrastructure. Their removal is cognitive dispossession.

+ Guattari's Three Ecologies: Explains why theft operates across multiple registers simultaneously. Environmental harm (disoriented routes), social harm (governance fragmentation), mental/cognitive harm (broken knowledge transmission) are inseparable.

+ Neidich's Cognitive Mapping: Translates ANT's network description into a politically legible format showing which actants are under community control vs. institutionally captured.

+ PAR's Epistemic Non-Substitution: Ensures ANT's symmetry principle protects community authority. When a community member disputes an ANT analysis, their interpretation is not feedback—it is the epistemologically primary account. ANT symmetry applied to researcher accountability means community authority supersedes researcher authority.

What Transfers Across Nomadic Contexts

The ANT framework is designed as a translation engine applicable across nomadic heritage globally:

What transfers: The principle of symmetry, the five protocol structure, the mapping methodology, the approach to void sites as active actants rather than absences.

What must be co-designed: Which actants matter in each context (Arctic routes vs. Andean sites have different actant ontologies), local cosmological vocabulary, jurisdiction-specific legal frameworks.

Each application engages a new nomadic community from the ground up. The ANT architecture is not imposed—it is adapted.

Legal & Policy Implications

ANT analysis produces evidence-based arguments for reframing international heritage law:

1. Relational cultural property: Juridical standing extended to the heritage assemblage as a whole, not only the extracted object. This changes repatriation proceedings under UNESCO 1970 Convention and UNIDROIT 1995 Convention.

2. Void sites as legal entities: The absence created by theft becomes a recognized harm with legal standing. Prosecution can extend beyond the extraction point to address network-wide accountability.

3. Non-human testimony as evidence: Landscape features documented through photovoice and assemblage mapping become admissible evidence in legal proceedings, expanding the evidentiary base beyond written documentation.
