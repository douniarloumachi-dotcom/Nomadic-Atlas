ANT Assemblage — The Heritage Network
What is this?
This map shows the heritage site as a living network of actants — both human and non-human. Actor-Network Theory (ANT) gives equal status to every entity that has effects: a rock-art panel, a water source, a migration route, a seasonal wind, and an elder co-researcher all appear as equivalent nodes with equal agency.
Why does this matter?
Conventional heritage law protects objects. ANT shows what is actually at stake: a network of relationships. When a panel is stolen, the loss is not just the object — it is the restructuring of every connection it sustained. A route loses its anchor. A water source becomes harder to find. A season loses its marker. The void is still an actant.
Key question: What is actually destroyed when rock art is stolen?

Neidich Cognitive Map
What is Neidich's cognitive mapping?
Warren Neidich argues that under cognitive capitalism, communities' ways of knowing are progressively replaced by externally legible representations — UNESCO categories, art market valuations, tourist narratives. The community's own cognitive map is overwritten. This view makes that overwriting spatially visible as a political argument.
How to read the two axes
↕ Vertical — degree of institutional capture:
Bottom = knowledge fully under community control.
Top = knowledge fully captured or substituted by external institutions.

↔ Horizontal — Guattari's three ecologies:
Left = Environmental (landscape, material actants).
Centre = Social (routes, practices, governance).
Right = Mental/Cognitive (cosmology, memory, meaning).
Key question: How far has each domain been captured — and can it be reclaimed?
Arrow directions
Dashed upward — extraction or substitution. Cognitive capitalism moves knowledge toward institutional capture, replacing community meaning with market or institutional value.
Solid downward — PAR-ANT restitution. The research works to return epistemic authority to the community: reclaiming vocabulary, co-producing governance tools.
The core argument, visualised
The void site sits top-left — fully captured by the market, removed from community control. The green arrows show where ANT-PAR works to pull knowledge back down: not by recovering the stolen panels, but by asserting community epistemic authority over what remains.

Controversy Map — The Full Theft Network
What is controversy mapping?
Latour's controversy mapping traces every actant that makes a theft economically viable — not just the extractor, but the full downstream network: dealers, auction houses, collectors, and the legal frameworks that allow provenance to be falsified. Click the void site node to follow the chain.
Why existing law is insufficient
Prosecution currently targets the extraction point. But extraction is economically rational only because the downstream network exists. Dealers, auction houses, and collectors sustain the market — and are rarely prosecuted. Controversy mapping produces the evidence base to extend legal accountability to every node in the chain.
Key question: Who makes rock art theft profitable, and how can each node be held accountable?
Research output
Output 2: prosecution-ready dossiers for INTERPOL's Works of Art unit. Each dossier traces one theft from extraction site to current location, naming every actant in the chain.

Governance Layer — Who Owns the Knowledge?
Why a governance layer?
Most academic research extracts knowledge from communities and processes it through frameworks the community cannot control. This governance model is derived directly from the theoretical commitments of the research — not added as ethical compliance.
What makes this model different
PAR's epistemic non-substitution: community interpretive authority is ontologically prior to the researcher's framework. When a co-researcher vetoes an interpretation, it is not feedback — it is the epistemologically primary account. ANT's symmetry applied to the researcher: her own institutional power must be made visible and accountable.
Key question: Who has the right to speak about a community's own knowledge?
The veto — why it is not procedural
A veto is not a complaint. When exercised, it becomes a primary research finding — documenting the exact point where community authority superseded the researcher's framework. It is recorded in the thesis as such.

Governance Layer — Data Sovereignty
Exactly who owns each piece of research data, who can publish it, and who can withdraw it
The architecture below is derived from the theoretical frameworks of this research. PAR's epistemic non-substitution means community authority is prior to the researcher's. ANT's symmetry means the researcher's own institutional power must be made transparent. Free, Prior and Informed Consent is the procedural floor; the five theoretically-derived ethical commitments are the ceiling.
The veto mechanism — why it is not a procedure
When a community co-researcher rejects an interpretation, that rejection is the epistemologically primary account — not feedback to be weighed. The veto documents the moment community authority superseded the researcher's framework. It is recorded in the thesis as a primary research finding. Any protocol vetoed is revised or withdrawn without defence.
Why community-owned?
Full sovereignty
Assemblage maps, controversy dossiers, co-analysis records. Communities are knowledge producers, not data sources (PAR). Researcher holds access copies only with explicit permission; publication requires community co-authorship.
Why jointly held?
Co-authored
Where researcher and community readings diverge, both are published with community reading given precedence. This is epistemic non-substitution in writing — the researcher does not get the last word on community knowledge.
Why subject to review?
Researcher reflexivity
ANT symmetry applied to the researcher: she is an actant with power derived from European institutional affiliation. The journal makes that power visible and accountable, published alongside findings.

What "void actant" means
A void is not an absence — it is an active force. The extracted panels continue to structure the network through their absence: a route loses orientation, a season loses its marker, knowledge-transmission chains break. The void's effects are as real as the panels were. This is the central legal and ontological argument of the research.

Why this is not just a practical problem
Guattari — Environmental Ecology
The route is an actant in the environmental ecology. Its disruption cascades into the social ecology (governance, collective decision-making) and the mental ecology (intergenerational transmission).
